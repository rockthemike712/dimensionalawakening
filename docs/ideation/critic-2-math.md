# CRITIC 2 — MATHEMATICS / IMPOSSIBILITY LENS

Question applied to all 25: **is the impossibility real, and is the rule true?**
Not "does it sound deep." Does a structure generate the surprise, does the same
rule keep paying, and does it compose with fold / observe / the dim shift — or
does it sit beside them wearing a label.

Calibration: 5 = competent. 7 = rare. 9 = ship it tomorrow.

## A1 — The Short Way Around
The math is real and it is the right math: concentrated Gaussian curvature at a
cone point, and a geodesic self-intersects exactly when the cone angle drops
below pi. Crucially the rule *keeps paying* — pinch more wedges, the deficit
accumulates, straight walks close into loops you cannot escape by going
straight. "A seam is a wedge of angle zero" is a genuine generalisation of the
existing verb rather than a second verb, and the reframe (you edited adjacency,
not existence) retro-explains the very first fold, which is the most valuable
kind of revelation available to this project. Two real problems: at FOV 4 from
directly overhead a shallow cone is invisible, and nine seconds of walking to
meet your own footprints is an eternity on a phone.

## A2 — What You Watch Backs Away
The "hidden math" is `e^{k·tau_gaze}` slapped on the metric. That is a rule, not
a geometry — no invariant, nothing conserved, nothing composes out of it, and
the number is whatever you tune it to. Worse, the misunderstanding *contradicts*
the rule instead of deepening it: "walking never mattered, the room re-rolls when
you look" tells the player the thing they learned was a lie, which is the cheap
version of the handoff's second realisation. And an unreachable goal is the
oldest way to make someone close a tab; the pitch's own risk section knows it.

## A3 — The Doubling Door
The fabrication is specific: a similarity quotient of ratio 2 has no bottom and
no top, so "closed at 2^8, 256x is 1x" is not that quotient — it is a teleport
after eight doors wearing the word "periodic." A mathematician spots it in one
breath. It also eats itself: if scale is genuinely a ring, the crack in the
skirting board *is* the hall, so it cannot also be hand-authored with a light
inside it. Legible and pleasant in the hand, and Manifold Garden and Patrick's
Parabox already did the good version.

## A4 — Left Comes Back Right
Correct, classical, and mechanised: the two routes really do sit in different
homotopy classes, and the fold *physically exhibits the identification* — the
abstract gluing becomes a piece of tape you can watch land. That is the cleanest
"the math is the mechanic" statement in Lens A. It dies on clarity. Swapping the
d-pad's left and right on a phone is indistinguishable from a bug, the player's
avatar is a chiral-free disc so the flip has nothing to read on, and the
companion spark is a patch over a hole, not a design.

## A5 — The Room in the Stone
"Containment is a 3-cycle" is a set-theory joke (a non-well-founded set) with no
machinery under it — it is a level loop, authored, and nothing derives from it.
The bridge-from-six-steps is a displacement map, not a structure, so the label
"hidden math" is decorative in the strictest sense. The pre-emptive floor
deformation is a scripted paradox with one intended reading. Cause separated
from effect by a scene transition, at L cost, on a phone, for one gag.

## B1 — Lower the Lamp
`s = L.y/(L.y − P.y)` going to infinity and flipping sign is genuinely the real
projective line, and — rare in this document — the impossibility is *visible*:
the shadow races past the horizon and returns from behind the lamp. Every child
has already run this experiment on a bedroom wall, so clarity is free. Two
deductions: the eyes-closed body-swap is bolted onto the projection rather than
implied by it, and "you were the shadow, the camera was standing in the light"
is the third of four ideas here that end with the same camera reveal.

## B2 — The Second Shadow
"Hidden math: the camera is an ordinary node in the scene graph" is an
implementation detail promoted to a theorem. There is no structure, no
invariant, no second payout — it is one reveal, and after the reveal the player
owns an orbit camera on a six-inch screen, which is the worst control in mobile
games. The one genuinely good idea is buried: folding hinged at the observer's
ground point means *where you look from decides what folds together*. Extract
that and throw the rest away.

## B3 — Built Behind Your Back
Frustum containment as an existence predicate is a switch statement, not
mathematics — nothing composes, nothing escalates, the rule pays exactly once.
It is also the oldest gag in the medium (Boo, the Weeping Angels, Antichamber's
corridor) and it re-skins the observation logic the existing double-slit room
already runs, without adding a single new idea to it. The second act ("it was
the frame, not your eyelids") is a good sentence attached to thirty seconds of
holding a button in the dark. Dead.

## B4 — One Right Place
"The solution is one point in R^3, not a shape in it" is correct and it is the
right kind of elegant — an anamorphic set really is a fibre of the projection.
The escalation is honest: the second set resolves from where no body goes, the
third from inside a pillar, so the same rule forces the camera and then the fold
into play, and the reframe deepens instead of contradicting. But this is the
most thoroughly mined seam in the genre (Superliminal, Viewfinder, Moncage), and
pixel-hunting a point in R^3 with a thumb is a chore that the sonification
mitigates rather than solves.

## B5 — Blink and Keep It
"The fold operator conjugated by the projection" is a beautiful sentence and it
does not typecheck: a projection is not invertible, so there is no pullback and
no defined answer to "what does folding the photo do to the world." The pitch
knows — its honest version is one scripted room, one hinge, three known objects
that move, which is precisely the one-developer-intended-trick the handoff
forbids. The final beat (the opening sheet was itself a photograph; something
blinked) is the single best revelation in all 25, and it is currently a promise
with no mechanism.

## C1 — Knock
`t = d/c` is real but shallow, and the "advanced solution of the wave equation"
is dressing on a negative number. The actual gold is elsewhere and the pitch
almost undersells it: **delays computed through foldedPoint() make the fold
audible before the player knows what folding is.** That is the metric made
perceivable, cheap, and it composes forwards. Best fun-per-hour in the set: tap
the floor, the world answers, no goal required. Loses points because the rule
pays out twice and then stops.

## C2 — Shut
Two orthogonal slices of one 4D cell complex is a real claim honestly cashed —
a wall in one slice is a door in the other *because* they are cross-sections of
the same face, which also pays the cross-section grammar D1 and D5 want. And it
contains the best composition proposed anywhere in this document: fold with your
eyes shut and you fold only the heard room, so you can drag a heard door
alongside a seen door and step through both. That is fold x observe producing
something neither does alone, and it generates levels rather than a solution.
The "eye sticks shut until you walk clear" turns a collision bug into a
mechanic. Weakest part is the vague third-shadow misunderstanding; heaviest
build in its lens.

## C3 — Where the Wobble Stops
`f = 200 + 5d` gives beat frequency `5|d_A − d_B|`, zero beat is the
perpendicular bisector, three pillars give the circumcenter, a field gives a
Voronoi diagram you walk with your feet. Every one of those is true, none of it
is announced, and the deepening is textbook-correct: the rule was never "same
distance," it was "simple ratio," so Apollonius circles and the 3:2 curve carry
bridges the bisector cannot reach. Same rule, four payouts, each geometrically
different. Folding slides the silent line under your feet, which means you can
drag the bridge to you. It is playable with the screen face down — that is proof
the mechanic is the mathematics and not a picture of it. Marked down only
because "walk until the hum stops" is a hot-and-cold hunt with better feedback,
not a toy.

## C4 — Tune
The label is false. Holonomy is a property of a loop in the *base* — here the
world twists after a loop in the *fibre* while the player stands still, which is
not holonomy, it is a scripted rotation with a bundle-theory hat on. "Pitch and
hue are the same number" is a poetic identification, not a mathematical one.
Strip the label and what remains is a global hue slider that toggles which
props are solid, which the pitch's own risk section admits is boring if the
off-hue world is empty. This is the one idea in the 25 that is literally "look at
all these crazy colours" — the exact thing the handoff's most important
principle rules out.

## C5 — Eight Steps
The Z/8 quotient is legitimate and folding-the-seam-halves-the-loop is a real
composition — folding space folding time, mechanised, not metaphor. But the
core is a dexterity gate in a game whose entire thesis is perception rather than
power, and the proposed fix guts it: if you fit the tempo and phase to the
player's own first eight steps, there is no beat to match, only a mirror. Add
Bluetooth latency on iOS Safari and the honest widened window is +/-140 ms with
no failure state, at which point rhythm is a decoration on walking.

## D1 — The Second Point
The best thing here is real: the breathing wall is a rotating rectangle and the
camera pitch-up proves it, so the player learns to read cross-sections before
anyone says the word — and that debt gets collected by D3 and D5. But it is a
prologue, not a mechanic. Its own text concedes "exactly one puzzle," which is
the forbidden shape, and nobody plays a prologue twice; the second time they
skip it. Twelve seconds of tapping in the dark plus fifty seconds of waiting for
a gap is not fun in the hand, it is a cutscene with a button.

## D2 — The Corner Comes to You
This is the correct answer to "make the existing game deeper." SO(3) is
non-abelian; two folds about perpendicular hinges compose to a rotation about a
diagonal axis; order matters, and a gate that only accepts a corner in one
orientation turns non-commutativity into a physical fact discovered by hand. A
child says "I did it in the wrong order." A mathematician sees the group. Nobody
writes a symbol. It is the existing verb composed with itself — the shader
already runs two sequential hinges — and it *can only exist after the birth of
depth*, so the fold gets deeper because the player gained a dimension, not
because they gained an ability. That is the thesis, mechanised, in one beat. The
mirrored double standing inside you, moving with opposite handedness because it
arrived through an odd number of reflections, is real reflection-group content
and the strongest image in the document. Real costs: doubly-folded geometry is
visual soup, and foldedPoint() must now match a genuinely 3D order-dependent
transform or the player collects lights they are not touching.

## D3 — Turning the Door
Rotation in one 2-plane of R^4 selects a hyperplane, the four rooms are four
axis-aligned slices, and 45 degrees is the diagonal cut nobody built — that last
detent is the correct deepening, converting "there are four rooms" into "there
is one object and the frame chooses where you cut it," which is the same lesson
D1 and D5 are betting on. Turning the frame with your eyes closed yielding the
uncommitted blend is *implied* by the grammar the existing double-slit room
already taught, not bolted on, and that coherence is worth a lot. It is also
days of work: portal render targets on a phone, and a blended room where two
wall sets are simultaneously solid is a collision problem that will read as
graphical corruption before it reads as R^4.

## D4 — The Flat Places
Degrees of freedom as dimension, with a lower-dimensional embedding satisfying
constraints its parent cannot — true, simple, and nearly free given every visual
already keys off `dim`. The d-pad losing keys is the strongest possible use of
an existing control and requires no teaching. The inversion (you can *choose*
to collapse, failure was a verb all along) is a genuine deepening and it answers
the handoff's dimensional-collapse question properly. But E3 is the same
mathematics with a better toy and a better second act — E3's second column
deletes a *different* axis, which is a free choice of the projection's kernel;
D4 has no equivalent. And taking a player's buttons away on a phone is the
fastest quit in the business.

## D5 — The Rising Cut
A foliation with each level a preimage under one SDF is real, and the
recognition beat — you know these rooms because you lived in them — needs no
words. The problem is structural: this cannot exist until the game does, and the
one-day version explicitly removes the walking, which reduces it to a museum
slider, i.e. a revelation that is only a reveal. There is also a quiet
tautology: the honest construction lofts the object *from* the level floorplans,
so "every level was a slice" is true by construction rather than discovered,
and that difference is exactly what a mathematician feels.

## E1 — Corners
The simplex ladder is genuinely on-thesis: each vertex adds a rotational degree
of freedom, so progression is literally degrees of perception rather than power,
and the fold composition is excellent — a flat 3-corner body slides under the
crease, a 4-corner body cannot lie flat, so pulling the seam bends *you*. Then
the impossible moment is a lie: a rigid tetrahedron does not pass a vertex
through its own interior under any element of SO(3), and its shadow never has
five corners. The pitch admits it needs a scripted two-second event. And the
fifth-corner-is-the-observer is the fourth camera reveal in this document.

## E2 — Octave
The teleport *is* the quotient map. Identify f ~ 2f and two pillars twenty units
apart become one object — that is not a metaphor for octave equivalence, it is
octave equivalence, executed as geometry. Then the deepening, which is the best
one here: ring a fifth instead of an octave and no gluing happens; a third
pillar grows between them, because 3:2 generates a new element rather than
collapsing one. Correct, discovered by doing, and it reframes the field as one
vibrating object whose pillars are overtones without contradicting a thing. It
hands the game a second, independent notion of "near" — pitch adjacency
alongside fold adjacency — so puzzles can demand either or both. And it is fun
in four seconds with no goal attached: brushing a line of reeds and playing a
phrase is the only thing in these 25 a person would do for its own sake. Cheapest
real win available: the 22 landmark cylinders already carry `{m,x,z,h}` records
and only need their heights quantised to the harmonic series instead of
`0.5 + random*1.8`.

## E3 — Thin
A projection with a kernel, where *which* coordinate is annihilated is a free
choice of the matrix. The second column deleting a different axis is the correct
deepening: it upgrades "flatten squashes me" into "flatten chooses which
coordinate the world loses," and the consequence — two rooms you visited
separately are now one room, and a light you already collected is here again,
uncollected — is derivable from the fibre identification rather than scripted.
Fold then flatten and the two halves project onto each other so crossing costs
nothing; that composes. It also makes dimensional collapse a toy the player
chooses before it is ever a punishment, which is a smarter answer to the
handoff's failure section than D4's. Genuine risk: if nothing impossible is
enabled within the first two entries it is a camera gimmick and gets walked past
forever.

## E4 — Two Turns
SU(2) double-covering SO(3) is beautiful and it is attached to a mechanic that
contradicts it. The sign of a spinor is famously *not observable* — that is the
whole point — so making it flip your shadow and your d-pad is not the Dirac belt,
it is a parity toggle with a physics label. Then it is the second idea to invert
the controls (see A4) and the fourth to end on a camera reveal. The one salvage:
pulling the seam while mirrored makes the far half arrive flipped, so the fold
pairs each place with its reflection — a genuinely different fold for no new
geometry code. Take that line, bin the belt.

## E5 — Beads
Best toy in the document, full stop — a tray of ball bearings with a granular
hiss is fun before the player understands anything, which is exactly the brief.
And one beat is superb design: fold the sheet, tilt, and the beads pour across
the crease without crossing the middle, teaching the fold's rule through play
*before* the player is told the fold does that. But gradient descent on a hidden
scalar field is a visualisation technique, not a structure — nothing composes
out of it beyond "folding changes downhill," and the field itself is authored,
so there is no rule that keeps paying. The misunderstanding is the weakest of
the four observer reveals: "there was never a landscape, it was you" contradicts
the surveying rule rather than deepening it, and retroactively makes the
player's cleverest work meaningless.

## TOP 5

1. **E2 — Octave.** The mechanic literally *is* the quotient map, the fifth
   generating a new pillar instead of a glue is a correct and unannounced
   deepening, and it is the only idea in 25 that is fun with no goal attached.
2. **D2 — The Corner Comes to You.** Non-commutativity of SO(3) delivered as
   "I did it in the wrong order," built out of the existing verb composed with
   itself, and impossible to stage before the birth of depth — the thesis in one
   beat.
3. **E3 — Thin.** A projection kernel you can choose, whose consequences (a gap
   with no width, two rooms identified into one) follow from the mathematics
   rather than a script, wrapped around a squash-and-pop toy players repeat.
4. **D3 — Turning the Door.** The highest real impossibility here, and the
   45-degree cut turns "four rooms" into "one object, and you choose the slice"
   — a deepening, not a contradiction.
5. **C3 — Where the Wobble Stops.** Zero-beat is a bisector, three pillars a
   circumcenter, and the true rule is "simple ratio," not "same distance"; the
   whole thing is playable with the screen face down, which proves the
   mathematics is the mechanic.

## KILL LIST

- **B3 (15)** — frustum containment is a switch statement, the gag is thirty
  years old, and it re-skins the observation logic the game already ships.
- **A2 (16)** — an ad-hoc conformal factor with no invariant; the twist
  contradicts the rule instead of deepening it, wrapped around an unreachable
  goal.
- **A5 (16)** — a displacement map and a level loop labelled as non-well-founded
  set theory; scripted causality gag at L cost across a scene transition.
- **C4 (16)** — the holonomy claim is false as described, and stripped of the
  label it is a hue slider that toggles solidity: the one idea here that is
  exactly "look at all these crazy colours."
- **E4 (16)** — a spinor's sign is unobservable by construction, so the visible
  mirror flip refutes the mathematics it cites; second offender on inverted
  controls.
- **B2 (17)** — "the camera is a scene-graph node" is not a theorem; one reveal,
  no second payout, and an orbit camera on a phone.

## ONE THING I'D BUILD FIRST

**E2 — Octave.** Quantise the 22 existing landmark heights to the harmonic
series, ring on contact, and identify octave pairs. Hours of work against code
that already exists, fun in four seconds with no goal, and it gives the game a
second notion of adjacency — pitch — that folding can then be composed against.
The fifth growing a new pillar proves the rule is a structure and not a trick,
and none of it touches the fragile two-gap room.
